# Homepage research map

**Research Delta** sits near the bottom of the homepage, immediately above Links. The closed view shows a narrow river panorama below a blue Research Delta heading. Clicking it unrolls the painting horizontally from the center over 1.15 seconds; project names, highlighted routes, filters, and details fade in toward the end. Closing reverses the motion. Native `details` / `summary` semantics remain for keyboard access and the no-JavaScript fallback. The small link beside Research Overview opens the map before jumping to it; loading or navigating to `#research-map` also opens it. Collapsing it manually keeps it closed until the next deliberate navigation or toggle. Typography now uses the homepage's Helvetica family and 24.5-pixel heading size, with its existing `#0088cc` link blue and lighter blue borders. The map's heading, filters, caption, and project details align with the homepage's 700-pixel text column; the expanded river keeps its wider canvas so the eight project names remain readable. This is a local homepage addition, inspired by the idea of an explorable publication map at <https://wrudman.github.io/>. The current version uses a simplified, flat river landscape generated with image_gen. The eight projects lie on river channels, with upstream-to-downstream connections following the water. Names sit beside their river markers for readability. See `research-delta-flat-prompt.md` for the current image prompt and provenance. No reference-site code or artwork is reused.

## Reading the map

- Branch controls use research-area names: Discourse & reasoning, LLM agents, AI4Science, and Language & cognition. The discourse branch covers the Overview's work on implicit structure, evaluation, and text applications; AI4Science corresponds to its scientific discovery direction. LLM agents also reflects the agent work described in Experience, and Language & cognition includes the bilingual speech paper. Project descriptions retain their specific contributions, such as ContextWeaver's memory construction method.
- Research areas can overlap. MQUD belongs to both Discourse & reasoning and AI4Science. The discourse filter includes QUD parsing, QUDeval, ElabQUD, QUDsim, QSalience, and MQUD. Selecting a research area highlights all its river segments; selecting a project keeps that area active when the project belongs to it. Under All work, project selection highlights the complete connected research line, including its forks and confluences. Relationship arrows remain specific to the selected project.
- Seven river segments share a QUD upstream channel. After QUD parsing, one branch passes QUDeval and divides toward QUDsim above and QSalience below. The ElabQUD branch joins at QSalience, which continues toward MQUD. QSalience no longer sits on the independent northern inlet. The underlying landscape contains additional unmarked river channels; those do not encode paper relationships.
- Six arrowed river paths indicate specific relationships, with explanations and source links in the project panel:
  - QUD parsing → QUDeval: evaluation of QUD parsing. Source: <https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.325/>.
  - QUDeval → QUDsim: QUDsim cites QUDeval in its QUD generation procedure (Section 4). Source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09373v2#S4>. This does not claim that QUDsim reuses the parsing code or evaluation metric.
  - QUD parsing → QSalience: a conceptual research connection between recovering questions answered by a text and estimating the value of answering reader questions. The author requested this connection. QSalience discusses QUD parsing in Section 2, but does not directly cite the 2023 parsing paper in its v2 bibliography. Source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10917v2#S2>. No implementation inheritance is claimed.
  - QUDeval → QSalience: QSalience follows QUDeval's constraint that questions be grounded in their anchor sentence when judging validity. Source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10917v2#S3>.
  - ElabQUD → QSalience: QSalience uses elaborative simplification to motivate deciding which concepts need explanations. This is an application motivation, not an assertion that QSalience reuses the ElabQUD model or dataset. Source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10917v2#S2>.
  - QSalience → MQUD: question salience extended to scientific utility (Section 2). Source: <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23733v3#S2>. This is a conceptual connection, not a code dependency.
- Language and cognition and LLM agents remain separate research themes. No paper connection is asserted between ContextWeaver or bilingual disfluencies and the QUD papers.
- Years and venues come from the site's existing publication metadata. Horizontal spacing is schematic; it is not a continuous time axis.

## Behavior and maintenance

`research/inquiry-map.json` holds the curated descriptions, positions, and connections. `research/inquiry-rivers.json` holds the river curves and their project memberships. `scripts/build_inquiry_map.py` combines these with `research/projects.json` and updates only the marked homepage section. Run with `--check` to check that the rendered section is current. `scripts/trace_inquiry_rivers.py` traces the current landscape's blue water mask to regenerate the positions and curves; it analyzes the image without editing it.

Project nodes select a detail panel; its Project and paper links navigate normally. Command/control clicks on nodes retain normal link behavior. Selecting a project highlights its complete research line with a uniform river stroke; arrows show connections involving the selected project. Existing paper connections determine the connected line, and no new direct relationships are added. Shared stream membership groups the research lineage and does not assert that every upstream paper supplies a method to every downstream paper. Branch controls highlight the appropriate river segments and markers. On small screens, the landscape retains eight markers with 44-pixel touch targets; selecting a marker moves to its details. Keyboard activation, an announcement region, and reduced-motion support are included. The native disclosure also opens and closes without JavaScript; inside it, nodes remain ordinary project links and all detail text is available.

The 2026-09-07 readability pass keeps all eight project names visible at every breakpoint. Labels have a light, flat background, and the narrow layout gives them separate positions while keeping their markers on the original river coordinates. Years are omitted from small map labels; full paper titles remain available in native hover tooltips and the linked project pages. Background scenery and inactive route overlays are subdued. Labels also stay visible when another branch is highlighted.

The homepage's prior publication lists, introductory text, project links, metadata, and resource labels are preserved. `constructing-questions/`, the Japanese homepage, and the eight project pages are unchanged by this addition.

The topology revision uses explicit river junctions and bends so the QUDsim and QSalience branches take different channels around the central island.
